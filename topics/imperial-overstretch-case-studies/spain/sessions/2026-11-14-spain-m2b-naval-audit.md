# CASE-ESP-001 — M2b naval cost and accounting audit session (2026-11-14)

**Canonical:** https://github.com/fweiger/AI_book/issues/32 ; existing Draft PR #36. No new issue.

## Research question
Can matched naval expenses and mainland colonial-related receipts prove that the late Bourbon empire became a fiscal loss-maker, and can an observed fall in navy spending be assigned to debt service?

## New analytical evidence and counterexamples
- Barbier's mainland naval share of total General Treasury spending: 1784–92 25.03%, 1793–96 31.51%, 1797–1801 14.32%, 1802–04 11.13%, 1805 7.66% (period weighted by spending, not average annual percentage).
- Cádiz five-year receipt/peninsular navy expense ratios: 61.54%,77.38%,79.72%,43.61% in 1760–79 (Indies-only proxy); 56.09%,108.15% in 1780–89 (Indies + Rentas), NOT uniform category across 1778 legal change. Ratios are not colonial net profitability.
- Marichal's constructed “Indias” numerator includes an attributed 25% Tobacco and 30% General income for 1783–1807; his 1791 229.686m reales vs Prados narrower direct remittances 161m is an **accounting definition difference**, not independent replication of the same metric. Do not misinterpret 1808 fiscal gaps as genuine state income zero.
- Original study acknowledges that 1784–1805 government expenditure/income debit totals are close mechanically despite differing accounting years; this is not evidence absence of deficits or funding costs.
- Author-reported Canga Argüelles 1793–95 war financing cost ~4,741m and ordinary tax cover ~40%: suggest financing strain, but real debt interest cannot be extracted from this single aggregate.
