# CASE-ESP-001 — M2 research session 2026-11-14

Question: Can 1784–1807 Madrid-linked fiscal receipts be compared annually with Spanish exports to Hispano-America, and does war-era disruption show a monotonic imperial collapse?

Source: Prados de la Escosura, L. (2024), *The Loss of the American Empire*, ch.6, DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-60792-9_6 ; publisher full-text and full tabular transcription visible on public chapter reproduction. Published chapter CC BY 4.0.

Actual changes: 43 consecutive fiscal years, 43 trade years in M2 CSVs; M2 analysis, source registry, updated README/changelog. Numerical calculations performed from GitHub-fetched table values: six segment means/shares, 1784–1807 correlation 0.6363, subsample correlations 0.0599/0.8921; simple arithmetic annual checks with rounding tolerance.

Interpretation: recurring war-period trade and fiscal receipt collapses are observed but non-monotone; 1802–1804 rebound contradicts an irreversible 1797 collapse. Colonies can cease Treasury remittances while trade markets partially survive; 1808 fiscal coverage discontinuity and trade geographic-imputation assumptions prevent clean causal differences.

Counterexample: Spain's fiscal-military accounts during eighteenth-century wars partly resemble Britain and Spanish debt interest share may have been lower. Civil administration crowd-out must not be taken as established without fiscal expenditures.
