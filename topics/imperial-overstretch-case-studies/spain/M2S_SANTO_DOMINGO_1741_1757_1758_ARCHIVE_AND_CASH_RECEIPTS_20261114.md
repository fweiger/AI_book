# CASE-ESP-001 · M2s — Santo Domingo 1741/1757 imperial payroll liquidity primary-locator audit

**Date:** 2026-11-14. **Authority:** [AI_book #32](https://github.com/fweiger/AI_book/issues/32); existing [Draft PR #36](https://github.com/fweiger/AI_book/pull/36); same Spain research branch. **Stage:** published table reconstruction and provenance/archival collection access audit, not original manuscript verification.

## Executive conclusion

**New:** A third published work by Gutiérrez Montoya & Marchena (2017) explicitly says **1741 and 1757** were the two armed mutinies of the Batallón Fijo, citing **AGI Santo Domingo 1042, 1092**. Julián (2017) cites **AGI Santo Domingo 1088, 1092** for the **1757** episode. Marichal & Souto (2012) date what may be the same arrears episode to **1758**, citing **AGNM Reales Cédulas Originales vol78 exps104/109** and **Historia vol570 f39**. None of these original manuscripts was accessible in this run; **do not resolve the date by majority vote**. Moreover Julián cites prior work by Marchena: apparent three-paper agreement is partly a **shared research genealogy**, not three independent original archive reads.

A new **1742–1750 8-annual-entry reception panel** has been copied from Julián 2017, published original-account certification summary; integer-peso total exactly **1,349,179** (part-fraction records not revalued). This provides a *pre-1757* liquidity-replenishment historical descriptive control, rather than evidence of 1757 wages themselves.

This is a **published quotation/secondary representation of a first-party dated certificate**; our agent has not viewed AHN manuscript folios.
2. Same paper p39 cites **1756-04-20** arrival at **bahía de Ocoa** of frigate *La Flecha*, carrying **154,585 pesos, 1 tomín, 5 granos**, allocated in the scholar's discussion to prior **1753/54** garrison wages (cites Sevilla Soler 1980 p240). **No onshore signed soldier pay sheets viewed**, so this is documented historical arrival claim, not actual salary settlement proof.
3. Same pp40–41 reports 1757 approx **150–180 soldiers**, armed occupation, negotiation, proposed partial three-month wages and six pesos monthly relief, followed by pardon. Authors' footnote29 cites **AGI Santo Domingo 1088 and1092** and three older secondary works. The **fraction actually paid** is not established from original vouchers.
4. [Gutiérrez Montoya & Marchena Fernández (2017)](https://revistas.grancanaria.com/index.php/CHCA/article/download/9988/9494), printed p20 footnote79, names 1741 **and** 1757 uprisings, and cites **AGI Santo Domingo1042 and1092**. Co-author Marchena is repeatedly cited in Julián/Marichal, so this **strengthens archive routing** but does not create independent primary corroboration.
5. [Marichal & Souto (2012)](https://carlosmarichal.colmex.mx/en/finanzas-coloniales/Nueva%20Espa%C2%A7a%20y%20financiamiento%20del%20imperio%20espa%C2%A7ol.pdf) printed pp27–28 footnote64 says 1758/10+months arrears, consequent **25,000 pesos increase ordered** and installments towards older arrears, with **AGNM Reales Cédulas Originales vol78 exps104/109; Historia vol570 f39**. Neither **date of original 1758 order** nor **actual payment to soldier** has been inspected. Does not prove separate event vs Julián1757.
6. The city's earlier **1741** crisis references an additional money-source alternative: short-term advances from local residents, *vecinos*, to pay troop expenses pending imperial remittance; Julián's narrative cites historical archives for governor's reported lack of treasury cash. **Local private loan vs central colonial transfer must be split**, and 1741 must not be counted as an independently identical intervention-control group.
7. **1748 'suspension' caution:** Julián p40 quotes an earlier historical explanation of a 1748 order suspending certain situado payments, but his pp24–25 also list **1748 and1749 actual received entries**. Their scope, target treasuries and duration are UNKNOWN; cannot represent this as a universal 1748-1757 zero-transfer policy without the original order.

## 3. Account-causality microstructure and explicit rivals

Proposed causal network:

`New Spain treasury approved situado` → `Veracruz/ship dispatched` → `Havana in transit` → `Ocoa/Santo Domingo received and classified` → `treasury wage payroll accrued` → `wages actually signed paid` → `military protest`.

**Observed from published studies:** partial dated arrivals, published 1751 certification and secondary descriptions of two protests. **Missing:** month-level same-flow matched departure/arrival/account entries, treasury cash balances, soldier-by-soldier accrued vs paid, original **1748 order**, archival folio dates of 1757/1758.

Rivals:
- **Shipping/war hazards:** vessel scarcity and maritime seizures could delay remittance.
- **Budget allocation and accounting:** funds moved under earmarks, other island fortification and navy demands, intermediary military vs civilian treasury books.
- **Local loan capital:** merchants, army officers, cattle duties, lending or negotiated advances temporarily bridged the missing cash.
- **Governance/distribution dispute:** governor or officials with cash but delayed/disputed payments; original vouchers would distinguish.
- **Labor market or discipline:** military revolt may reflect accumulated arrears plus coercive leverage, not just current liquidity.

No statistical sample large enough for causal regression exists in this case yet. **A single 1757 armed event cannot estimate fiscal-crisis hazard-rate without a credible panel of paid/unpaid garrisons over multiple years.**
